3 Security Measures You Should Implement To Protect Your E-mails and Blog

Posted on July 2, 2021 | Written by Cheptiony Mutai | Category: Business Tips, Online Security

Online security has drastically changed in the post-pandemic era, making robust defenses essential for every freelancer and business owner. The threats are real: a report by the National Technology Security Coalition highlighted the exposure of over 770 million email addresses in a single 2019 breach, confirming that **email remains the top attack vector**. Today, phishing extends beyond the inbox, targeting mobile devices, social media, and gaming platforms.

To help you stay secure, I'm sharing **three critical security measures** I implemented to protect my emails, blog, and overall freelance business from cyber threats.

1. Implement Digital Declutter: Unsubscribe from Marketing Emails and Delete Dormant Accounts

This first step is deceptively simple but incredibly powerful. Every account you create represents a potential **data exposure point** and every unwanted email is a distraction. By reducing your digital footprint, you drastically reduce your attack surface for data breaches, spam, and targeted phishing attempts.

The core strategy is simple:

  • **Unsubscribe Ruthlessly:** Aggressively unsubscribe from all marketing emails that don't directly contribute to your business or personal growth. A clean inbox improves **focus** and protects you from spam filters misdirecting legitimate client emails.
  • **Close Dormant Accounts:** If you haven't logged into an online service, forum, or e-commerce site in over a year, delete the account. I personally made this decision with accounts like Amazon and Udemy that were no longer essential to my current focus.

This process is about embracing a lean, high-value digital environment. As a creator, your mental clarity is your most valuable asset.

You may want to learn more on why protecting your private data is important by checking this article here – Don’t Sign Up Everywhere Using Your E-mail and Phone Number. Here’s Why.

It takes courage to clean your inbox and close the door on digital clutter. But doing so provides a clean slate, allowing you to focus your attention and creative energy where it truly belongs: on building your business.

2. Make Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Mandatory for All Logins

Initially, I hesitated, thinking complex passwords were enough. I quickly learned that **2FA is the most critical defense layer** against account takeover. Even if a hacker steals your password, they cannot access your account without the second factor (usually a unique code generated on your phone).

I highly recommend enabling 2FA for all high-value accounts, including:

  • **Email Providers:** Gmail, Outlook, etc. (The primary hub for all resets).
  • **Freelancing Platforms:** Upwork, Fiverr, etc.
  • **Financial Accounts:** Banks, payment processors, and crypto wallets.

I personally use the Microsoft Authenticator app, as recommended by my domain provider, and it has been instrumental in keeping my professional email secure since 2021.

3. Secure All Digital Assets with SSL Certificates

In the modern web, websites using **HTTP** are flagged by browsers like Google Chrome as **"Not Secure."** This immediately erodes user trust and can even impact search engine ranking.

The simple and necessary fix is securing your site with an **SSL (Secure Sockets Layer) certificate**.

An SSL certificate ensures that your website URL begins with **https** and displays the crucial lock icon. More importantly, it encrypts the data exchanged between the user's browser and your site, protecting confidential information from theft and ensuring the secure delivery of emails sent through your contact forms.

This measure is a non-negotiable step toward establishing professionalism and protecting your clients' data.

Conclusion

For any successful online business, investing in security isn't an option—it's foundational. By sealing these basic loopholes, you prevent scammers from derailing your focus and making your digital environment stressful. Always prioritize the implementation of recommended security features that support your long-term growth.

Remember to focus only on activities and tools that generate genuine **value and security**.
