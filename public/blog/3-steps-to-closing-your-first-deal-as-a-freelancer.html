3 Steps To Closing Your First Deal as a Freelancer

Posted on February 6, 2019 | Written by Cheptiony Mutai | Category: Freelancing Tips

If you're starting as a freelancer, you know how challenging it is to secure that first deal, especially when competing with hundreds of established freelancers. The fear of failure before you've even started is real.

The core goal, however, remains simple: to make more sales, and eventually, focus on high-value contracts. To help you get your footing, here are **3 powerful steps to closing your first deal**, even if you have zero prior experience.

1. Research Your Client's Requirements and Address Them Directly

This may seem obvious, but it's where most beginners fail. Serious clients are looking for a quick solution from someone who understands their pain points—not a lengthy career history. In your cover letter or proposal, stick precisely to the topic of the job description.

In the freelance world, nobody cares if you have 10 years of experience; they care about who can solve their problem **fast** and **effectively**. Tell them exactly how you plan to tackle the job. **Be brief, be confident, and demonstrate specific understanding.**

2. Offer to Speak to Your Prospect on Call

Once a client shows interest in your proposal by messaging you, schedule a call immediately. Proposing a call is a sign of confidence and professionalism. It’s also convenient for a busy client who wants to avoid lengthy email exchanges.

A call lets you demonstrate your commitment, clarify requirements in real-time, and build trust—a crucial component in the client-freelancer relationship. Most clients are willing to get on a call at a time that works for you, as it speeds up their hiring process.

3. Ask Them to Officially Close the Deal to Start Work

This step, while sometimes unusual, works best: **don’t wait for the client to close the deal; ask them to do so once you have all the necessary information.**

  • Be polite but firm about setting a deadline.
  • Never start working without a contract or funds placed in escrow (on platforms like Upwork). Starting without a secured commitment is extremely risky and can lead to non-payment.

The act of the client putting money into escrow demonstrates **commitment** and allows you to start the project knowing your time and effort will be protected.

Conclusion

These three steps—being specific in your proposals, building trust through communication, and securing the deal officially—are the foundation for a successful freelance career. Mastering them early will dramatically increase your closing rate.
